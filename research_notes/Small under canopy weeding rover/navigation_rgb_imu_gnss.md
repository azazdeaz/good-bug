## 1. GNSS under canopy: degradation at 10-25 cm antenna height, low-cost RTK receivers (price and accuracy), coarse-localization value, polytunnels

### Takeaway
No source measures RTK from an antenna 10-25 cm above ground under pepper or maize canopy. The closest evidence is UIUC's sorghum-row test with a high-end Trimble antenna (errors above 0.4 m, with drift), together with forest-canopy RTK studies (fix rate about 88% but metre-level RMSE, plus wrong fixes that look valid). This supports using GNSS only for coarse tasks: field and headland geofencing, picking the row (only if the error is below half the row spacing, which under-canopy conditions do not guarantee), and return-to-base. Lateral control should come from vision. Dual-frequency modules now cost about $60-260. Polytunnel plastic attenuates GNSS far less than glass or metal greenhouses, but metal frames still cause multipath.

### Cited Findings
**Under crop canopy (row crops)**
- A UIUC under-canopy LiDAR navigation preprint (JFR) reports that a small robot driven inside sorghum rows had RTK-GNSS errors **above 0.4 m even with a high-end Trimble antenna**, with occasional accuracy drops and drift. The authors attribute this to multipath and signal attenuation. — [Higuti et al., Under canopy LiDAR-based autonomous navigation (JFR preprint)](https://terra-mepp.illinois.edu/sites/terra-mepp.illinois.edu/files/2021-06/JFR_under_canopy_lidar_preprint.pdf) (from a search snippet; the PDF host was blocked here)
- The same line of work (a figure caption on ResearchGate) states that GNSS, even with RTK, does not give reliable localization for within-row ground robots, and that the TerraSentia corn/sorghum robot does not use RTK/GNSS for row navigation. — [ResearchGate figure, small robot under canopy](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/a-A-small-robot-completely-under-canopy-was-manually-driven-in-the-middle-of-3m_fig1_329599219)
- The UIUC/Bonn AdaCropFollow precursor (arXiv 2410.12411, Oct 2024) explicitly cites "RTK-GPS degradation under the canopy" as the motivation for vision-only under-canopy navigation. — [AdaCropFollow arXiv 2410.12411](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12411v1); [Lamarr Institute listing](https://lamarr-institute.org/publication/adacropfollow-self-supervised-online-adaptation-for-visual-under-canopy-navigation-2/)
- Contrast case (open arable field, mast-mounted antenna, not under canopy): an RTK module gave about **33 mm cross-track accuracy**, consistent with its spec. — [de Silva et al., A Vision-Based Navigation System for Arable Fields, arXiv 2309.11989](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.11989)

**Under tree canopy (closest quantitative analogue)**
- RTK under tree canopy: **88.2% fixed-solution rate** across scenarios, but horizontal **RMSE about 2.03 m (MAE 1.63 m)** overall, against about 3.1 cm in open conditions. A "fixed" flag therefore does not guarantee accuracy under canopy. — [Evaluation of the Repeatability and Accuracy of RTK GNSS under Tree Canopy (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/391566844_Evaluation_of_the_Repeatability_and_Accuracy_of_RTK_GNSS_under_Tree_Canopy)
- A structured review gives indicative ranges of roughly **0.5-2.03 m** for RTK under dense canopy, and says these are not statistically aggregated. — [GNSS Positioning Accuracy in Forest Environments: A Structured Critical Review (Geodetski list)](https://ojs.srce.hr/geodetski-list/article/view/44106)
- Survey-grade RTK in forest with short observation times: canopy effects were statistically significant, and DOP affected precision but not accuracy. — [Performance Evaluation of RTK-GNSS ... in Forested Areas (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11479190/); [preprint](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202408.1605)
- Multipath is described as the most important error source under canopy. Receivers can produce bad fixed solutions that look acceptable unless verified. Signals that pass through canopy are weak. — [rtkdata, RTK multipath fix dropouts](https://rtkdata.com/rtk-multipath-fix-dropouts/); [Lefixea, RTK surveying under trees](https://www.lefixea.com/article/rtk112); [Javad note](https://internal.javad.com/jgnss/javad/news/pr20150812.html)
- Leica/FIG work reports that advanced multipath estimation and interference mitigation substantially improve RTK fix availability and reduce wrong-ambiguity-fix errors in difficult conditions. — [Leica white paper](https://leica-geosystems.com/-/media/files/leicageosystems/products/other/improved%20highprecision%20rtk%20positioning%20through%20multipath%20reduction%20and%20interference%20mitigation%20as%20pu.ashx); [FIG 2022 abstract](https://www.fig.net:443/resources/proceedings/fig_proceedings/fig2022/papers/ts08c/TS08C_gonzalez-calvo_luo_et_al_11557_abs.pdf)

**Low-cost receivers under foliage**
- u-blox ZED-F9P: static positioning under tree canopy with adapted RTKLIB reached sub-decimetre accuracy (static, long observation; not a moving rover). — [Sensors 23(6):3136, Static Positioning under Tree Canopy Using Low-Cost GNSS Receivers](https://doi.org/10.3390/s23063136)
- A 2025/2026 study of vegetation and season effects found that the **ZED-F9P had lower mean horizontal error than the single-band MAX-M10S in every canopy condition and vegetation period** tested. — [Evaluation of Accuracy and Usability of Low-Cost GNSS Receivers Under Tree Canopy (MDPI Geomatics 6(2):34)](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-7418/6/2/34); [ResearchGate copy](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/403280440_Evaluation_of_Accuracy_and_Usability_of_Low-Cost_GNSS_Receivers_Under_Tree_Canopy_Impact_of_Vegetation_and_Seasonal_Changes)
- No published under-canopy test was found for the Unicore UM980/UM982 or the Quectel LC29H. — (search result, see Gaps)
- Quectel LC29HEA (L1/L5 dual-frequency RTK): **$57.69 for receiver plus antenna, + $4.79 shipping** (Apr 2024). With a better antenna the setup cost about **$81** (Aug 2024). In an open-sky comparison it "did not perform as well as the u-blox F9P with a more expensive antenna", but results were "more similar" when antennas of equal quality were used. — [rtklibexplorer, Dual-frequency RTK for less than $60](https://rtklibexplorer.wordpress.com/2024/04/28/dual-frequency-rtk-for-less-than-60-with-the-quectel-lc29hea/); [rtklibexplorer, LC29HEA with improved antenna](https://rtklibexplorer.wordpress.com/2024/08/01/quectel-lc29hea-with-improved-antenna/)
- ArduPilot community users (anecdotal) rated the LC29 as "marginally better than an M8P and significantly worse than an F9P, UM982 or Mosaic X". They also reported sudden satellite-count drops with fallback to RTK-float, and noted that the LC29HEA does not output hAcc (accuracy estimate) messages. — [ArduPilot Discourse, RTK GPS on a budget (LC29HEA)](https://discuss.ardupilot.org/t/rtk-gps-on-a-budget-quectel-lc29hea/117606); [page 9](https://discuss.ardupilot.org/t/rtk-gps-on-a-budget-quectel-lc29hea/117606?page=9); [page 13](https://discuss.ardupilot.org/t/rtk-gps-on-a-budget-quectel-lc29hea/117606?page=13)
- Price snapshots (undated retailer listings, treat as approximate): SparkFun ZED-F9P GPS-RTK-SMA breakout $259.95; gnss.store ZED-F9P boards about $189-245; ArduSimple simpleRTK2B (F9P) from 172 EUR; ArduSimple simpleRTK3B (UM980) about 223 EUR (the snippet reading was uncertain); a UM980 Raspberry Pi HAT exists at gnss.store. — [SparkFun](https://www.sparkfun.com/sparkfun-gps-rtk-sma-breakout-zed-f9p-qwiic.html); [gnss.store ZED-F9P](https://gnss.store/collections/zed-f9p-gnss-modules); [ArduSimple RTK receivers](https://www.ardusimple.com/rtk-receivers/); [gnss.store UM980 RPi HAT](https://gnss.store/en/high-precision-rtk-gnss-modules/337-elt0229.html)
- Datasheet-level claims: a comparison site lists the UM980 at 0.8 cm + 1 ppm horizontal, and SparkFun advertises the ZED-F9P at "10 mm 3D accuracy". These are open-sky figures and are not comparable like-for-like. The same comparison site (vendor-affiliated) claims dual-frequency receivers in general degrade quickly under canopy. — [gnss-solutions.com UM982 vs ZED-F9P vs mosaic-X5](https://gnss-solutions.com/best-septentrio-gnss-alternatives-u-blox-f9p-vs-unicore-um982-vs-mosaic-x5/); [SparkFun GPS-RTK2](https://www.sparkfun.com/sparkfun-gps-rtk2-board-zed-f9p-qwiic-gps-15136.html); [rtkdata, How to choose a GNSS receiver in 2026](https://rtkdata.com/blog/how-to-choose-a-gnss-receiver/)

**Polytunnels and greenhouses**
- Standalone (non-RTK) GNSS: ETFE polytunnels give **decimetre-level positioning** and track more satellites than glass greenhouses. The authors conclude polytunnels are "more suitable for precision agriculture applications relying on standalone GNSS". — [Study of Standalone GNSS Positioning Precision ... ETFE Polytunnels vs Glass Greenhouses (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/401524977_A_Study_of_Standalone_GNSS_Positioning_Precision_in_Covered_Agricultural_Environments_ETFE_Polytunnels_Versus_Glass_Greenhouses)
- A secondhand citation reports "severe GNSS performance degradation in greenhouses with signal attenuation of 15-20 dB and frequent outages caused by metal structures and coated glass" (original source not accessed). — same search, [ResearchGate figure "Typical greenhouse environment"](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Typical-greenhouse-environment_fig2_338523712)
- A 2026 track-mounted pollination robot paper says GNSS "can sometimes be unreliable under protective structures due to signal attenuation and reflections caused by metallic frames and coverings". It used RFID tags as the primary localization and GNSS only as a supplement. — [AGRICAM, arXiv 2608.29237](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.29237)

### Inferences
- (Inference) A 15 x 20 cm rover's antenna sits at 10-25 cm, below all or most of the canopy and close to wet soil and leaves. That is a worse geometry than UIUC's sorghum test (TerraSentia's mast is higher) and close to the worst forest cases. Plan for float or no-fix most of the time in mid- and late-season peppers or maize, and occasional *wrong fixes* with 0.5-2 m error. Never close the lateral control loop on GNSS in-row.
- (Inference) Still useful: (1) at headlands and in open early-season fields, RTK fixes are probably available and can re-anchor the map and row index; (2) metre-level float or standalone positions are enough for a field geofence, "which end of the field am I at", and return-to-charger approach. Row indexing by GNSS alone needs error below half the row spacing (for example below 25-40 cm for 50-80 cm pepper rows), which under-canopy data does not support. Count rows visually or by odometry and use GNSS as a sanity check.
- (Inference) The LC29HEA (about $60-80 including antenna) is the cost-sensible choice for a tiny rover where GNSS is optional and coarse. A ZED-F9P (about $190-260) or UM980 is justified only if headland and open-field RTK accuracy matters. Antenna quality and ground plane dominate results (rtklibexplorer). A tiny rover limits ground-plane size, which worsens multipath.
- (Inference) In polytunnels, use the metal hoops/frames and the regular bed geometry as landmarks (or AprilTags on hoops) rather than GNSS.

### Gaps
- No measurement found of GNSS/RTK fix rate or error with the antenna at 10-25 cm under pepper, maize or soy canopy. EarthSense/UIUC report qualitative failure and >0.4 m in sorghum, not a systematic study.
- No under-canopy or under-foliage test found for UM980/UM982 or LC29H. No verified 2026 price list. Prices above are undated retailer snapshots (2024-2026).
- No RTK fix-rate study inside plastic polytunnels with metal hoops (only standalone GNSS in ETFE).
- Full text of the Higuti et al. JFR preprint could not be fetched (host blocked), so exact error statistics are unverified.

## 2. Vision-only row following under canopy: CropFollow family, Bonn visual servoing, classical ExG/Hough, segmentation, learned end-to-end, foundation models on Jetson

### Takeaway
The best-validated under-canopy camera-only approach is UIUC's line: CropFollow (2021, ResNet-18 heading and offset regression plus MPC, 485 m per intervention, beating a LiDAR system at 286 m) and then CropFollow++ (2024, three semantic keypoints, 25 km deployed on commercial cover-crop planting robots, 13 vs 33 collisions in late-season tests). Successors in 2024-2026 address domain shift (AdaCropFollow, test-time adaptation using foundation-model features; MetaCropFollow) and gaps (LeCropFollow, 2026, latent-space MPC with TD-MPC2 on a Jetson Orin Nano at 20 Hz, 2.4x fewer semantic failures than keypoints in gaps). Bonn's crop-agnostic visual servoing (BonnBot-I, 3.82 cm average error against teleoperation) is the main above-canopy alternative. Classical ExG + Hough runs at about 30 FPS at 320x240 even on an RPi 3B, but is brittle and assumes a top-down view of rows, which does not hold under canopy.

### Cited Findings
**CropFollow (RSS 2021, UIUC, Sivakumar et al.)**
- Monocular RGB from low-cost cameras, learned perception and MPC. **485 m per intervention** on average against **286 m for a state-of-the-art LiDAR system**, over **>25 km** of field testing. — [RSS 2021 paper page](https://roboticsconference.org/2021/program/papers/019/index.html); [RSS PDF](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss17/p019.pdf); [arXiv 2107.02792](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.02792)
- ResNet-18 on a front-facing camera predicts heading and a distance ratio. Best model: **L1 error 1.99 deg heading, 0.04 distance ratio**. Another paper version reports 2.20 deg / 0.07 (a version discrepancy). The camera sensor is OV2710 plus an IMU. A results table lists "CropFollow (w/ IMU) @ 22 FPS" (hardware for that figure not confirmed). CropFollow was "vastly superior" to VINS-Fusion in cross-track distance. — [ResearchGate CropFollow](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/353043076_Learned_Visual_Navigation_for_Under-Canopy_Agricultural_Robots); [Gupta lab PDF](https://saurabhg.web.illinois.edu/pdfs/sivakumar2021learned.pdf)
- A third-party description: "2 ResNet-18 NNs take **320 x 240** RGB images as input and estimate the heading angle and distance-to-edge". — [ApproxCaliper, MLSys 2023](https://proceedings.mlsys.org/paper_files/paper/2023/file/89efa87dc8f0a5d18e4ae0a479658f60-Paper-mlsys2023.pdf)

**CropFollow++ (2024)**
- Replaces regression with **three heatmaps: vanishing point, left-row intercept and right-row intercept**. Heading and lateral offset are computed from the keypoints using camera intrinsics and **IMU roll**. It is more modular and interpretable and gives a **confidence measure for occlusion detection**. — [Lessons from Deploying CropFollow++, arXiv 2404.17718](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.17718); [Moonlight review](https://www.themoonlight.io/en/review/lessons-from-deploying-cropfollow-under-canopy-agricultural-navigation-with-keypoints)
- RSS 2024 demo: **13 vs 33 collisions** (CropFollow++ vs CropFollow) over about 1.9 km each in challenging late-season fields with heavy occlusion. — [RSS 2024 paper 23](https://roboticsconference.org/2024/program/papers/23/); [RSS proceedings p023](https://roboticsproceedings.org/rss20/p023.html)
- Deployed on **multiple under-canopy cover-crop planting robots, 25 km total**. Lessons: in gaps the network can mistake a *neighbouring* row for the current one; more training images from other viewpoints would help; heatmap visualisation helped explain interventions. When a crash was detected the robot stopped and ran a crash-recovery procedure. **Lane turns were done manually.** — [arXiv 2404.17718](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.17718); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.17718v1)

**2024-2026 successors**
- **AdaCropFollow** (Sivakumar, Magistri, Behley, Stachniss, Chowdhary; IROS 2026 version "Self-Supervised Test-Time Adaptation"). It uses **vision foundation model features** plus a geometric-prior pseudo-labelling criterion to adapt the keypoint model **onboard, with no human labels**, from a small amount of unlabeled target-domain data. Experiments ran on a **Jetson AGX Orin 64 GB**. The 2024 precursor focused on avoiding catastrophic forgetting. — [IROS 2026 PDF (Uni Bonn)](https://www.ipb.uni-bonn.de/wp-content/papercite-data/pdf/sivakumar2026iros.pdf); [arXiv 2410.12411](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12411v1)
- **MetaCropFollow**: few-shot meta-learning adaptation for under-canopy navigation. — [arXiv 2411.14092 (fugumt mirror)](https://fugumt.com/fugumt/paper_check/2411.14092v1_enmode)
- **LeCropFollow** (Tommaselli et al., USP + UIUC, arXiv 2606.31941, 30 Jun 2026). Skips explicit geometric state estimation. A self-supervised semantic heatmap extractor feeds a **TD-MPC2 model-based RL planner that plans in latent space over the raw heatmap**. Late-season corn: matched baselines on unstructured rows and outperformed them in planting gaps, with a **2.4x reduction in semantic failures vs keypoint methods**. Zero-shot sim-to-real. Hardware: **TerraSentia skid-steer, ZED 2i camera (right monocular stream only), Jetson Orin Nano, policy and planner asynchronous and throttled to the camera's 20 Hz**. Limitations: straight corn rows only, limits on physical feedback and gap geometry. Code: felipe-tommaselli.github.io/lecropfollow. — [arXiv 2606.31941](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.31941); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.31941v1); [HF papers](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.31941); [chatpaper](https://chatpaper.com/paper/305628)
- **RowDetr** (arXiv 2412.10525, Dec 2024): end-to-end polynomial crop-row detector for under-canopy images (detection only, no turning). — [arXiv 2412.10525](https://web3.arxiv.org/abs/2412.10525)

**Uni Bonn visual servoing (above or at canopy level, top-down-ish cameras)**
- Ahmadi, Nardi, Chebrolu, Stachniss (2019): a visual servoing controller steers from crop-row directional features tracked in a sliding window, with a row-switching mechanism. **A pair of cameras (front and back) lets the robot enter the next row in limited space without sharp turns.** Code, real data and simulation are public. — [arXiv 1909.12754](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.12754)
- Ahmadi, Halstead, McCool, BonnBot-I (IROS 2022, "Towards Autonomous (Crop-Agnostic) Visual Navigation in Arable Fields"): two monocular cameras (front and back), vision only, no GPS or map. **Average navigation accuracy 3.82 cm relative to manual teleoperation, across five crop types**, three fields and varied illumination. Lane switching uses SIFT features and counts rows while shifting, "independent of any global positioning system". — [arXiv 2109.11936](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.11936); [v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2109.11936v2); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/354857701_Towards_Autonomous_Crop-Agnostic_Visual_Navigation_in_Arable_Fields)

**Segmentation-based (Lincoln/Aberdeen, de Silva, Cielniak, Gao)**
- U-Net crop-row mask plus "triangle scan" post-processing extracts the central row line, with visual servoing. Row following worked across growth stages, but **gaps >1 m in rows required human intervention**. — [arXiv 2309.11989](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.11989); [Vision based crop row navigation under varying field conditions, arXiv 2209.14003](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.14003); [Deep learning-based crop row detection, arXiv 2209.04278](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.04278)

**Classical ExG / Hough and Pi-class throughput**
- A filtered Hough transform on an **RPi 3B reached about 30 FPS at 320 x 240** (Basso and de Freitas 2019, cited in a review). Other classical pipelines in the same review ran at 0.143-3.33 FPS. An ExG + Otsu pipeline inside a YOLO-predicted ROI took about 25 ms per 640 x 360 image (>40 FPS; hardware unstated). An ONNX deep row model ran at **about 10 FPS on a Raspberry Pi 5** at 0.18 m/s. Hough assumes straight rows and needs per-field and per-crop tuning. — [Real-time crop row detection using computer vision (PMC review)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11558879/); [Winterhalter et al. RA-L 2018](http://ais.informatik.uni-freiburg.de/publications/papers/winterhalter18ral.pdf); [Adaptive multi-ROI maize](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/05b3/f1460b6325136ec908b54bdd8e17aff3e127.pdf)

**Monocular depth and navigation foundation models on Jetson**
- Depth Anything V2 (metric) on **Jetson Orin Nano, TensorRT FP16: 10.1 ms at 392^2 (about 98 FPS engine-only), 17.9 ms at 518^2**. Self-reported, not peer-reviewed. — [kornia/vision-rt](https://github.com/kornia/vision-rt)
- Depth-Anything-Small on Orin 8 GB: **98 ms at 518 x 518, 23.5 ms at 308 x 308** (backend unstated). — [IRCVLab/Depth-Anything-for-Jetson-Orin](https://github.com/IRCVLab/Depth-Anything-for-Jetson-Orin)
- On AGX Orin (more powerful): DAv2-S 11.5 ms, B 26.8 ms, L 84.5 ms. — [Which Depth Estimator for Jetson AGX Orin? arXiv 2610.27199](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.27199v1); [nodarhub/orin-depth-benchmark](https://github.com/nodarhub/orin-depth-benchmark)
- A Depth Anything 3 ROS 2 wrapper exists. — [GerdsenAI DA3 ROS2 wrapper](https://github.com/GerdsenAI/GerdsenAI-Depth-Anything-3-ROS2-Wrapper)
- Tiny distilled monocular depth: DepthART (2026). — [arXiv 2607.17099](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.17099)
- GNM / ViNT / NoMaD general navigation foundation models: the official code includes deployment on a LoCoBot with a **Jetson Orin Nano**. NoMaD needs ">15x fewer parameters" than ViNT's subgoal-diffusion system, making it practical on Jetson Orin. No Orin Nano FPS published. A zero-shot real-world evaluation repo exists. — [visualnav-transformer GitHub](https://github.com/robodhruv/visualnav-transformer); [NoMaD arXiv 2310.07896](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.07896); [ViNT](https://general-navigation-models.github.io/vint/); [vnm-zeroshot-eval](https://github.com/MaevaGuerrier/vnm-zeroshot-eval)

### Inferences
- (Inference) For a 15 cm wide rover, CropFollow++'s formulation (vanishing point plus two row-base intercepts from one forward camera, IMU roll for geometry) transfers best. It was designed for a camera *under* the canopy looking along the row, which matches this rover. The Bonn and Lincoln methods assume a downward or oblique view of rows from above canopy height and are better suited to early-season or low crops.
- (Inference) Compute: a ResNet-18 at 320 x 240 or a small heatmap network is feasible on an RPi 5 at about 5-15 FPS with NCNN/ONNX INT8 (extrapolated from about 10 FPS ONNX row-model on Pi 5), and comfortably at >20 Hz on an Orin Nano (LeCropFollow runs a heavier pipeline at 20 Hz there). At typical small-rover speeds of 0.1-0.3 m/s, 5-10 Hz perception is probably enough. Unverified; benchmark on target hardware.
- (Inference) The training-data gap is the main risk. All UIUC models were trained on corn/sorghum/soy from a TerraSentia viewpoint (camera height probably higher than 10-20 cm; see Gaps). Expect to collect your own pepper data. AdaCropFollow-style self-supervised adaptation (foundation-model features plus geometric priors) is the 2026 recipe for cutting labelling, but it was run on an AGX Orin, not a Nano or Pi.
- (Inference) Monocular depth foundation models (DAv2-S at about 40-100 FPS on Orin Nano at low resolution) could provide free-space and stem-proximity cues and a collision guard, but no paper was found that uses them for under-canopy row following. Treat as experimental. Not practical on an RPi at useful rates (no source found).
- (Inference) VLA or LLM-based navigation is not justified for in-row following on this hardware. No ag-specific deployment was found. NoMaD-class models could at most serve for docking or return-to-base topological navigation, as an experiment.

### Gaps
- The exact TerraSentia camera height, FOV and onboard compute for CropFollow (22 FPS figure) and CropFollow++ could not be verified (full texts blocked). Recollection (unverified): TerraSentia is about 0.3 m wide, so the rover here is smaller.
- Lateral error in cm for CropFollow++ and LeCropFollow under canopy was not found in snippets. Most UIUC metrics are interventions or collisions per distance.
- No published under-canopy row following in peppers/paprika, or at 10-20 cm camera height, was found.
- No source found on end-to-end (pixels-to-steering) or VLA models deployed under canopy on Pi- or Nano-class hardware.

## 3. Row-end detection, headland turns and row switching without LiDAR (camera + IMU + wheel odometry)

### Takeaway
This is the least mature part of the stack. Even UIUC's 25 km commercial deployment of CropFollow++ did lane turns **manually**. Camera-only switching has been shown mainly above canopy (BonnBot-I with front and back cameras and SIFT row counting; Lincoln's row-exit and switching, which still used a depth camera at the row end). Reported errors: 24 cm average (up to a 50 cm margin) for row exit, and 92.5% coverage with 2.5% overlap in a simulated full-field run, with failures dominated by rotation errors and odometry in the headland. For a tiny rover, engineered aids (AprilTags or coloured stakes at row ends, a known row spacing, IMU-heading-controlled U-turns) are the pragmatic choice.

### Cited Findings
- CropFollow++ deployment: a run ended if an intervention happened before the row end, and **manual lane turning** was then performed. — [arXiv 2404.17718](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.17718v1)
- UIUC LiDAR under-canopy work: "turning at the end of the row from one row to another requires significant further development." — [JFR preprint](https://terra-mepp.illinois.edu/sites/terra-mepp.illinois.edu/files/2021-06/JFR_under_canopy_lidar_preprint.pdf)
- **Learning to Turn** (UIUC, arXiv 2408.03059): diffusion-policy imitation learning for under-canopy row turning, trained from human or privileged-controller demonstrations. **Shown only in simulation (corn field)** "with only visual inputs". — [arXiv 2408.03059](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.03059); [Lacuna summary](https://lacuna.tiptreesystems.com/work/learning-to-turn-diffusion-imitation-for-robust-row-turning-in-under-canopy/wrk_67dfaa67cce01802c1f72055d44415d1)
- BonnBot-I: feature-based (SIFT) lane switching that detects and counts rows while shifting, with no GNSS. — [arXiv 2109.11936](https://arxiv.org/html/2109.11936v1)
- Ahmadi 2019: front and back camera pair allows entering a new row "within a limited space without sharp turns" (the robot shifts laterally using the rear camera after exiting). — [arXiv 1909.12754](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.12754)
- de Silva, Cielniak, Gao (arXiv 2309.11989, v1 2023, v2 2024): RGB segmentation **plus depth data** detect the row end and the re-entry point of the next row. They note that in existing methods "the initial row switching direction must be manually specified". The vision-based transition step had the highest errors. A simulated field-scale run achieved **92.5% field coverage and 2.5% row-traversal overlap**. Skipping and overlap were caused mainly by **rotational errors during switching**, and headland navigation relied mainly on **wheel odometry**. — [arXiv 2309.11989](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.11989); [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=FTtWqQochF)
- **Leaving the Lines Behind** (arXiv 2306.05869): RGB local-feature-matching visual feedback to exit the row, **depth image at the row end** to estimate headland travel distance. Overall **average error 24 cm** in combined vision-only experiments, and the robot left the row "with an error margin of 50 cm". — [arXiv 2306.05869](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05869)
- Fiducials at row ends: a strawberry-field system placed markers at the end of each bed and used their image coordinates to cross to the next row. AprilTags at row starts plus ultrasonic collision sensing enabled minimum-time row-transition control. — [Minimum-time row transition control of a vision-guided agricultural robot (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/357001386_Minimum-time_row_transition_control_of_a_vision-guided_agricultural_robot)

### Inferences
- (Inference) Row-end detection without LiDAR for an under-canopy camera: (a) CropFollow++-style keypoint **confidence collapse** and a vanishing point that disappears or brightens (open sky or headland ahead); (b) a sudden rise in image brightness or sky fraction; (c) loss of stem detections on both sides over N frames; (d) odometry distance against the known row length (from a teach run or GNSS); (e) an AprilTag or coloured stake seen at the row end. Fusing at least two of these is advisable. No paper gives a reliability figure for camera-only under-canopy row-end detection.
- (Inference) For headland turns, a small skid-steer rover can pivot in place, which removes most of the "limited space" problem the Bonn papers addressed. The remaining issue is landing in the correct next row. Recommended: exit by a fixed odometry distance, rotate 90 deg on IMU yaw (gyro yaw over a few seconds is accurate to about 1 deg with a calibrated MEMS IMU; this is general knowledge, not sourced here), drive the known row spacing (counting rows visually if a side camera sees them), rotate 90 deg, then re-acquire the row with the in-row perception and verify via keypoint confidence. Fall back to a ground AprilTag at each row mouth.
- (Inference) Rotational error during switching was the dominant error source in de Silva's simulation. For a 15 cm rover in 20-60 cm gaps, a 5 deg heading error over 50 cm of row spacing already gives about 4 cm lateral offset, which is acceptable only if re-acquisition is robust.

### Gaps
- No field reliability statistic (success rate per turn) for camera-only row switching under canopy. Learning to Turn is simulation-only, and the Lincoln work used depth at the row end.
- No 2025-2026 under-canopy row-turning field paper was found.

## 4. VO / VIO / visual SLAM in crop fields: accuracy on ag datasets and real-time feasibility on RPi 4/5 vs Jetson Nano/Orin Nano

### Takeaway
General VIO/SLAM remains fragile in fields. On Rosario v2 (soybean, 7.3 km), ORB-SLAM3 lost tracking on some sequences, and with loop closure it **closed 7 loops incorrectly, irreversibly corrupting the trajectory** (repetitive crop texture causes perceptual aliasing). CropFollow beat VINS-Fusion decisively on cross-track error. Compute-wise, monocular ORB-SLAM3 runs at about 18-20 FPS on an RPi 5, and a GPU fork reaches about 28-32 FPS on an Orin Nano. For this rover, VIO is a short-horizon odometry aid with loop closure off, not a global localizer.

### Cited Findings
- **Rosario Dataset v2** (2025): about 7.3 km and 2.2 h of soybean-field operation in six sequences. Stereo IR, RGB, IMU, GNSS and wheel odometry, with GNSS-PPK + INS ground truth. Baselines ORB-SLAM3, ORB-SLAM3+GNSS and OpenVINS: some runs were partial because methods lost tracking (marked with asterisks). **ORB-SLAM3 with loop closure "incorrectly closes 7 loops and the estimated trajectory gets irreversibly corrupted"**. The authors write that "agricultural environments are still challenging for state-of-the-art SLAM systems". — [arXiv 2508.21635](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.21635)
- **Schmidt, Blessing, Enzweiler, Valada** (arXiv 2408.01716, with a later extended version): benchmark of ORB-SLAM3, VINS-Fusion, OpenVINS, Kimera and SVO Pro in agricultural settings, focused on loop-closing accuracy against compute on embedded systems. They release the first outdoor loop-closure benchmark dataset and reproducible code. Secondary summaries conflict (one says ORB-SLAM3 had the lowest ATE with loop closure, another says VINS-Fusion and OpenVINS give the best accuracy/efficiency balance), and neither was verified against the tables. — [arXiv 2408.01716](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.01716); [web3 mirror](https://web3.arxiv.org/abs/2408.01716); [Moonlight review](https://www.themoonlight.io/review/visual-inertial-slam-for-agricultural-robotics-benchmarking-the-benefits-and-computational-costs-of-loop-closing); code: github.com/iis-esslingen/vi-slam_lc_benchmark (from snippet)
- A 2025 IMAV paper proposed a hybrid ORB-SLAM2 with synthetic depth maps and reported better localization on Rosario. — [IMAV 2025 paper](https://www.imavs.org/papers/2025/2.pdf)
- CropFollow was "vastly superior" to VINS-Fusion on cross-track distance from the lane middle. — [ResearchGate CropFollow](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/353193713_Learned_Visual_Navigation_for_Under-Canopy_Agricultural_Robots)
- **Raspberry Pi 5:** only **monocular ORB-SLAM3 consistently ran in real time (about 18-20 FPS average)**. Stereo and inertial modes did not keep up. — [An In-depth Evaluation of ORB-SLAM3 on the Raspberry Pi 5 (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/400559478_An_In-depth_Evaluation_of_ORB-SLAM3_on_the_Raspberry_Pi_5_Performance_Stability_and_Design_Guidelines_for_Embedded_SLAM)
- RPi 5 RGB-D profiling (the RGB-D sensor is not allowed here, but the compute data is useful): ORB-SLAM3 uses 40.5 ms CPU per frame (26.7 ms critical path), end-to-end 26.1-61.9 ms per frame depending on resolution. RTAB-Map was also profiled. — [RGB-D SLAM on the Edge, arXiv 2611.16472](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.16472v1)
- **Jetson Orin Nano:** Jetson-ORB-SLAM3 (GPU fork, TensorRT CNN loop closure) runs **monocular-inertial at 32 FPS mean** on EuRoC. Stereo-inertial runs at **14.4 FPS stock CPU and 28.0 FPS** with ORB extraction overlapped with tracking. — [Jetson-ORB-SLAM3, arXiv 2608.17874](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.17874)
- VIO on Jetson boards was benchmarked on an MAV (older Jetsons, figures not retrieved). — [Run Your VIO on NVIDIA Jetson, arXiv 2103.01655](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.01655)
- Orin Nano is well ahead of Pi 5 on perception workloads (YOLOv8n) on a rover. — [Fictionlab, Orin Nano vs Raspberry Pi 5 on a rover](https://fictionlab.pl/blog/benchmarking-perception-workloads-on-a-rover-jetson-orin-nano-vs-raspberry-pi-5/)

### Inferences
- (Inference) Under canopy at 10-20 cm height, conditions are worse than on Rosario (soy seen from a larger robot): close-range leaves cause large parallax and motion blur, leaves move in wind, and there is little sky or horizon. Expect more tracking loss. Monocular VO without IMU scale will drift in scale. Monocular-inertial (ORB-SLAM3 mono-inertial, OpenVINS, VINS-Mono) needs good IMU-camera time sync and preferably a global-shutter camera.
- (Inference) Practical use: run VIO with **loop closure disabled** (Rosario shows false loops) as a relative-motion source fused with wheel odometry and IMU in an EKF (robot_localization), mainly for headland turns and for detecting wheel slip. On an RPi 5 this consumes most of the CPU (mono about 18-20 FPS), leaving little for the row network. On an Orin Nano both fit.
- (Inference) The repository already contains an `openvslam-wrap` directory (seen in the salvaged session's repo listing), so the team has prior OpenVSLAM/stella_vslam experience. No ag-specific stella_vslam benchmark was found.
- Not evaluated in any found source for ag: DROID-SLAM (too heavy for Nano/Pi in general; inference), Kimera (in Schmidt benchmark only), RTAB-Map mono.

### Gaps
- No quantitative ATE numbers (per method, per dataset) were retrievable for Rosario v2 or the Schmidt benchmark (full texts blocked).
- No VIO evaluation on the UIUC under-canopy data, FieldSAFE or Bacchus datasets was found in this session.
- No VINS-Fusion/OpenVINS FPS on Orin Nano or Pi 5 was found.

## 5. Wheel odometry + IMU dead reckoning drift for a small skid-steer rover on loose soil

### Takeaway
No source gives a field-measured slip or drift percentage for a small skid-steer rover on agricultural soil. Literature agrees that skid-steer odometry is biased by slip, especially in turns. The IMU gives good short-term heading, while position needs an external reference (visual row lock-on, VO, tags or GNSS).

### Cited Findings
- Wheel slip biases wheel odometry and causes dead-reckoning drift. Slip can be estimated as the mismatch between body velocity and wheel-odometry velocity (skid-steer tests on five surfaces, 2026). — [Floor Friction Assessment from Robot Tire-Surface Interaction, arXiv 2610.33735](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.33735)
- An IMU + encoder slip-estimation study on a skid-steered Pioneer AT on soil, sand, gravel and woodchips reports slip prediction error of about 20% of step size (definition not confirmed). — [IMU-based localization and slip estimation for skid-steered mobile robots (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/221065982_IMU-based_localization_and_slip_estimation_for_skid-steered_mobile_robots); [Terrain adaptive odometry for skid-steer robots](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/261416012_Terrain_adaptive_odometry_for_mobile_skid-steer_robots)
- Learned or online-calibrated kinematic models for skid steering (in LiDAR-IMU-wheel factor graphs) substantially improve odometry. These methods use LiDAR, but the online kinematic calibration idea transfers. — [arXiv 2404.02515](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.02515); [arXiv 2407.08907](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.08907v2)
- Practitioner notes: skid steering turns by scrubbing, so odometry error spikes in turns and on loose ground, and differential drive is more odometry-friendly. — [Fictionlab, Differential Drive vs Skid Steering](https://fictionlab.pl/blog/differential-drive-or-skid-steering-trade-offs-you-feel-only-in-the-field/)
- In de Silva's switching study, headland manoeuvres relying mainly on wheel odometry were a main source of row skipping and overlap. — [arXiv 2309.11989](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.11989)

### Inferences
- (Inference) Design implication: use **IMU (gyro) yaw, not differential wheel speeds, for heading**, especially in pivot turns. Use wheel encoders only for along-track distance, with a slip-scale factor calibrated per soil condition (or estimated online against visual odometry or row-stem passing rate). Expect several percent distance error on loose or wet soil and much larger errors in pivot turns (general robotics experience; no field-specific source).
- (Inference) For a 15 x 20 cm rover, a differential-drive layout with a caster or tracks may give better odometry than 4-wheel skid steer, at the cost of traction on clods. This needs a mechanical trade-off study elsewhere in the project.

### Gaps
- No measured drift (m per 100 m, or % of distance) for small skid-steer rovers on tilled or loose agricultural soil was found.

## 6. Low-viewpoint camera issues (10-20 cm height, occlusion, lens dirt, dappled and low light, HDR, shutter, FOV, forward vs downward) and artificial or natural landmarks

### Takeaway
Few sources address these issues quantitatively. Documented items: UIUC's CropFollow++ lessons (row confusion in gaps, need for viewpoint diversity, confidence for occlusion); lighting sensitivity of furrow/mulch detection by time of day; and AprilTag pose rotation errors at low resolution or in harsh light. AprilTags work well as row-end and greenhouse-pillar landmarks.

### Cited Findings
- CropFollow++: the confidence measure detects occlusion. In gaps the network can lock onto the neighbouring row. More training images from other viewpoints are recommended. — [arXiv 2404.17718](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.17718); [RSS 2024](https://roboticsconference.org/2024/program/papers/23/)
- LeCropFollow: irregular planting and gaps are the main failure mode of under-canopy robots, and geometric methods discard uncertainty and semantic context. — [arXiv 2606.31941](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.31941)
- Stereo ridge/furrow detection on plastic-mulched ridges: accuracy was >90% in daylight but **dropped abruptly at specific times of day** (illumination/specular effects). — [Stereovision-based ridge-furrow detection and tracking for auto-guided cultivator (COMPAG)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S016816992100507X)
- AprilTags with low-resolution cameras or harsh lighting "often produce poses with tremendous rotational errors". IMU fusion helps. — [Sensor Fusion for Fiducial Tags (IROS)](https://pengjujin.github.io/files/iros_aptag.pdf)
- Greenhouse: AprilTags on pillars in a factor graph counter feature degradation; tested at 0.2/0.4/0.6 m/s. — [Factor graph-based high-precision visual positioning for agricultural robots with fiducial markers (COMPAG)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S016816992200607X)
- AprilTag fused into a LiDAR-inertial graph as a GNSS substitute reported tag positioning errors within 3.0 mm in stability tests (LiDAR part not allowed here, tag part transferable). — [Agronomy 15(12):2731, LIO-SAM + AprilTag](https://doi.org/10.3390/agronomy15122731)
- Greenhouse photoreal simulation dataset with AprilTag detection in plant rows. — [GreenhouseSplat, arXiv 2510.01848](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.01848)

### Inferences
- (Inference, engineering judgement, not sourced): use a **global-shutter** sensor (for example OV9281/AR0234-class) or a high-frame-rate rolling shutter with short exposure, to avoid skew on bumpy soil and support VIO. Use a **wide FOV (about 120-160 deg)** forward camera tilted slightly down so both row bases and the vanishing point stay in view in 20-60 cm gaps. At 10-20 cm height, near leaves will fill the image, so a wide FOV keeps stems visible. Use a **downward or oblique second camera** for stem/weed detection and a short-range "am I centred between stems" cue. Rear camera optional (Bonn row-switching style). Use HDR or locked auto-exposure metered on the lower half of the image (soil and stems), not the bright canopy gaps. Add a hydrophobic cover glass and a shroud, and consider a simple lens-dirt detector (blur/variance check) that triggers stop-and-alert.
- (Inference) Low light under dense canopy suggests a small IR or white LED ring plus short exposure. The camera must then be sensitive (larger pixels). No under-canopy illumination study was found.
- (Inference) Crop stems as landmarks: pepper stems in a planted line form a regular sequence that can provide along-row odometry (stem counting) and row-centre references. Not validated in any found paper for navigation.

### Gaps
- No quantitative study found on camera height (10-20 cm) vs navigation performance, rolling vs global shutter in fields, fisheye vs standard lenses for row following, or lens soiling rates.
- No polytunnel-specific fiducial study.

## 7. Raised beds, ridges, drip lines and plastic mulch (paprika/pepper): do they help navigation?

### Takeaway
Plausibly yes, but evidence is thin and mostly from tractor-scale or stereo systems. Furrows and mulched ridges have been tracked by vision (98% furrow-line detection rate, about 3.8 deg angle RMSE; stereo mulched-ridge classification >90% but lighting-sensitive). For an under-canopy rover running in the furrow between raised beds, the bed walls and mulch edge are strong, high-contrast, crop-independent geometric cues. They could complement or replace stem-based row detection (inference).

### Cited Findings
- Vision-based furrow-line detection for a worker-assistance robot: **98.0% detection rate, 12.1 px RMSE in position, 3.8 deg RMSE in angle**, using a wide camera angle with a low depression angle. — [Vision-based furrow line detection (ScienceDirect)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1881836616301094)
- Stereo ridge-furrow detection on bare and plastic-mulched ridges: >90% classification in daylight, real-time guidance of a riding cultivator, accuracy drop on mulch at specific times of day. **Requires stereo** (allowed: stereo RGB). — [COMPAG 2021](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S016816992100507X)
- An RGB-D furrow-following robot measured furrow width and tracked ridges with fuzzy control. Ridges were hard to detect when occluded by a person ahead. **Requires active depth (not allowed).** — [Human-following control in furrow (Springer)](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-95892-3_12)
- Plastic mulch layers form raised beds about 5-9 in (13-23 cm) high and lay drip tape and mulch in one pass. — [WSU Small Fruits, mechanically laying biodegradable plastic mulch (2026)](https://smallfruits.wsu.edu/2026/01/05/mechanically-laying-biodegradable-plastic-mulch/)

### Inferences
- (Inference) For paprika on mulched raised beds, the 13-23 cm bed height is about the rover's camera height. A rover driving in the furrow sees the bed walls and black or silver mulch edges as two strong lines converging to a vanishing point. That gives the same geometry as CropFollow++'s left/right intercept plus vanishing point, but with much higher contrast and no seasonal change. A simple classical edge or colour-segmentation pipeline could work on an RPi. The furrow also mechanically constrains a small rover, giving a passive self-centring effect.
- (Inference) Risks: specular reflections from silver or white mulch (cf. time-of-day accuracy drop), mulch flaps, and drip tape or irrigation hoses lying in the furrow (obstacles or entanglement for a 15 cm rover). If drip lines run on top of beds under the mulch, they are not visible. If they run in the furrow, they could be followed as a line, but no paper was found.
- (Inference) If the rover instead runs *on top of* the bed between plant rows (weeding in-row), the planting-hole pattern in the mulch is a regular landmark grid.

### Gaps
- No paper found on small-robot navigation along plastic-mulch edges, drip lines or raised-bed furrows in peppers or vegetables using monocular RGB.

## 9. Practical navigation stack for a 15 cm x 20 cm under-canopy rover (in-row following, end-of-row detection, headland turns and row switching, global localization and coverage tracking, return to charger)

### Takeaway
(Synthesis; mostly inference grounded in the findings above.) A stack proven in pieces, though never as a whole at this size: a forward wide-angle global-shutter RGB camera running a CropFollow++-style keypoint network (vanishing point plus row intercepts, with confidence), fused with IMU yaw and wheel odometry in an EKF. Row-end detection from keypoint confidence, odometry and row-end fiducials. Scripted IMU-controlled headland pivots with visual re-acquisition. A topological field map (row index, distance along row) for coverage. GNSS only for coarse anchoring in open headlands. AprilTag-guided docking for the charger. Use an Orin Nano if learned perception and VIO must both run, and an RPi 5 only for a classical or very small network pipeline.

### Cited Findings (anchors for the proposed design)
- Learned monocular keypoints + MPC gave 485 m per intervention (CropFollow) and 13 vs 33 collisions in late season (CropFollow++), with 25 km deployed; turns were manual. — [RSS 2021](https://roboticsconference.org/2021/program/papers/019/index.html); [RSS 2024](https://roboticsconference.org/2024/program/papers/23/); [arXiv 2404.17718](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.17718)
- A 2026 heatmap + latent MPC pipeline runs at 20 Hz on a Jetson Orin Nano from a monocular stream. — [LeCropFollow arXiv 2606.31941](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.31941v1)
- Above-canopy, two-camera visual servoing with SIFT row counting switches rows without GNSS (3.82 cm accuracy). — [arXiv 2109.11936](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.11936)
- Odometry-reliant headland manoeuvres cause skipping and overlap (92.5% coverage in simulation). — [arXiv 2309.11989](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.11989)
- RTK under canopy: >0.4 m errors (sorghum), metre-level under trees with misleading fixes. — [JFR preprint](https://terra-mepp.illinois.edu/sites/terra-mepp.illinois.edu/files/2021-06/JFR_under_canopy_lidar_preprint.pdf); [RTK under tree canopy](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/391566844_Evaluation_of_the_Repeatability_and_Accuracy_of_RTK_GNSS_under_Tree_Canopy)
- ORB-SLAM3 loop closure in fields produced false loops (7 on Rosario v2). — [arXiv 2508.21635](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.21635)
- AprilTags are a proven row-end and transition aid. — [Minimum-time row transition (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/357001386_Minimum-time_row_transition_control_of_a_vision-guided_agricultural_robot)

### Inferences (proposed stack; all items are the researcher's inference)
1. **Sensors.** (a) Forward camera at the maximum height the chassis allows (15-20 cm), wide FOV (about 120-150 deg), global shutter, slight downward tilt, HDR or bottom-weighted auto-exposure, lens shroud and wiper or air-puff if feasible. (b) Downward or oblique camera for stems/weeds, which doubles as a close-range centring sensor. (c) Optional rear camera for Bonn-style re-entry and reversing out of dead-ends. (d) 6-axis IMU (gyro-based yaw), wheel encoders, and an optional LC29HEA or ZED-F9P on the tallest point.
2. **In-row following.** CropFollow++-style keypoint heatmaps (VP, left and right row-base intercepts) at 5-20 Hz, giving heading and lateral offset. Use a pure-pursuit or MPC controller with low speed (0.1-0.3 m/s). Gate on keypoint confidence: on low confidence, slow down, hold the IMU heading for a short distance (to bridge gaps), and stop if confidence stays low. In mulched or raised-bed peppers, add a classical bed-edge/mulch-edge detector as a second, independent estimator and cross-check the two.
3. **Row-end detection.** Vote between: keypoint confidence collapse with an open-field appearance, odometry distance against expected row length (learned on the first pass or from the GNSS map), a row-end AprilTag or coloured stake (most reliable), and GNSS headland geofence where a fix is available.
4. **Headland turn and row switch.** Scripted manoeuvre: drive out a fixed distance, then IMU-yaw pivot 90 deg, then translate one row spacing (counted visually by passing row ends or tags if possible, otherwise by odometry), then pivot 90 deg, then re-acquire the row using the keypoint confidence check. If acquisition fails, back up and search laterally. Optionally, a learned turning policy (Learning to Turn) later, once simulation-to-real is proven.
5. **Global localization and coverage.** Topological map: (field, row index, along-row distance). Row index comes from turn counting, verified by row-end tags with encoded IDs (an AprilTag ID per row is cheap and removes ambiguity). Along-row distance comes from odometry, corrected at each row end. GNSS, where available, re-anchors in headlands and flags gross errors. Optional short-horizon VIO with loop closure disabled. Coverage is logged as visited (row, segment).
6. **Return to charger.** Navigate the topological map to the charger's headland position, then dock on an AprilTag or ArUco board at the charger with visual servoing (proven greenhouse-style tag localization). Contacts are better than precise wireless alignment for a tiny robot.
7. **Compute.** RPi 5: classical mulch/edge pipeline plus a small INT8 network at about 5-10 FPS, with no room for VIO. Orin Nano: keypoint network, monocular depth guard and VIO concurrently (LeCropFollow at 20 Hz; Jetson-ORB-SLAM3 mono-inertial about 32 FPS on EuRoC). For a weeding rover that also runs weed detection, the Orin Nano is the safer choice.
8. **Software.** ROS 2 with robot_localization EKF, a custom row-follow controller node (not Nav2's planners for in-row), and Nav2 behaviour trees for the mission state machine (follow, then end-of-row, then turn, then re-acquire, then dock). Bonn ROS 2 port and LeCropFollow code as references.

### Gaps
- No end-to-end system of this size (15 x 20 cm) under canopy with published km-per-intervention was found. All quantitative results come from TerraSentia-class (about 30 cm+) or larger robots.
- No source on charger docking for under-canopy field robots.
- The proposed stack is untested; reliability of row-end detection and of turns is the main unknown and should be the first field experiment.
