## 1. Row spacing, in-row spacing, beds and free strip width in the main pepper systems

### Takeaway
Open-field pepper is grown either (a) on 0.73-1.02 m single rows/ridges/beds (Spanish pimentón, New Mexico chile, Central European field pepper), usually furrow- or drip-irrigated on bare soil, or (b) in US-style plasticulture: twin rows 30-46 cm apart on a plastic-mulched raised bed, on 1.5-2.0 m bed centres with a bare "row middle" between beds. In both systems the space between rows (or between beds) is far wider than the 15 cm rover; the geometric constraint is canopy overhang and low branches later in the season, not row width.

### Cited Findings
**US plasticulture (fresh bell pepper, transplanted)**
- Twin-row peppers on raised beds under black plastic with drip irrigation is the standard commercial setup in US extension guides; double rows increase plant populations and marketable yields — [Kentucky bell pepper guide](https://publications.ca.uky.edu/sites/publications.ca.uky.edu/files/bellpeppers.pdf); [Missouri G6372](https://extension.missouri.edu/g6372).
- Bed centres: Missouri 4-5 ft (122-152 cm); Penn State 5-6.5 ft (152-198 cm); Univ. of Georgia 60-72 in (152-183 cm) centre to centre; Manitoba twin rows on 5 ft (1.5 m) centres — [Missouri](https://extension.missouri.edu/g6372); [Penn State](https://extension.psu.edu/pepper-production); [Manitoba](https://gov.mb.ca/agriculture/crops/crop-management/pubs/bmz00s16p.pdf); UGA as summarised in salvaged search (UGA URL not in result list).
- Distance between the two rows on one bed: Missouri ~18 in (46 cm); Maryland 12-15 in (30-38 cm); Penn State 14-18 in (36-46 cm); Manitoba 17 in (43 cm) — [Missouri](https://extension.missouri.edu/g6372); [UMD 2026 Peppers](https://extension.umd.edu/sites/extension.umd.edu/files/2026-02/Peppers.pdf); [Penn State](https://extension.psu.edu/bell-pepper-growing-methods); [Manitoba](https://gov.mb.ca/agriculture/crops/crop-management/pubs/bmz00s16p.pdf).
- In-row spacing: many Kentucky growers use 12 in (30 cm); jumbo-fruit growers space wider; Maryland: plants staggered 12-18 in (30-46 cm) in each of the double rows. Kentucky twin-row system needs ~14,500 transplants/acre (~35,800/ha) — [Kentucky](https://publications.ca.uky.edu/sites/publications.ca.uky.edu/files/bellpeppers.pdf); [UMD](https://extension.umd.edu/sites/extension.umd.edu/files/2026-02/Peppers.pdf).
- Plastic width: Maryland 5 ft (152 cm) plastic for double rows, 4 ft (122 cm) for single-row peppers; Georgia: mulch needs an extra 20-24 in (51-61 cm) of width beyond the planting bed when planning bed spacing — [UMD](https://extension.umd.edu/sites/extension.umd.edu/files/2026-02/Peppers.pdf); UGA via salvaged search.
- Florida (Locascio & Stall 1994): one or two plant rows on 1.22 m beds and two or three rows on 1.83 m beds were tested on polyethylene-mulched beds; yield per plant was 50% greater with one row per 1.22 m bed than with two rows/1.22 m or three rows/1.83 m — [UF/IFAS EDIS HS368](https://edis.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/HS368) (as summarised in search result).
- High-tunnel example (South Carolina): two pepper rows per bed 14 in (36 cm) apart; for cages or Florida-weave trellising one row per bed at 18 in (46 cm) in-row; variety Sprinter 12-18 in in-row, 12-20 in between rows and "must be trellised, does best with Florida-weave" — [Richland County SC high tunnel guide](https://richlandcountysc.gov/Portals/0/Departments/Conservation/DistrictDocs/High%20Tunnel%202017/Spring%20Warm%20Season%20Crops%20in%20High%20Tunnel.pdf).
- Kentucky: a bed shaper/plastic layer and a transplanter that plants through plastic are essential for this system — [Kentucky](https://publications.ca.uky.edu/sites/publications.ca.uky.edu/files/bellpeppers.pdf).

**New Mexico chile (direct-seeded, furrow or drip, machine/hand harvest)**
- Most common row widths in New Mexico are 30-40 in (76-102 cm); narrower rows can give higher yields; growers often choose row spacing to match other row crops in the rotation (cotton, corn) — [NMSU Guide H-230](https://pubs.nmsu.edu/_h/H230.pdf) (via search extract).
- Chile is commonly direct-seeded and later thinned; thinning is typically 9-10 weeks after seeding; in-row spacings of 10 and 20 cm after thinning were used in NM weed-competition research — [Weed Technology 2023, pendimethalin in chile](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/976115DABDDFAC0957092D6C02380C2F/core-reader).
- NMSU plant-population research for machine harvest exists (CTF13) — [NMSU CTF13](https://pubs.nmsu.edu/research/horticulture/CTF13/index.html) (content not retrieved).

**Spanish pimentón (La Vera, Murcia/Ebro)**
- Ebro-valley pimentón-type crop: lines 0.73-0.76 m apart, "Agridulce" varieties, 70,000-75,000 plants/ha — [Univ. Extremadura 2012, mechanisation of pimentón pepper in La Vera](https://www.unex.es/conoce-la-uex/estructura-academica/centros/eia/archivos/iag/2012/2012_10%20Avances%20en%20la%20mecanizacion%20del%20cultivo%20de%20pimiento%20para%20pimenton%20en%20la%20comarca%20de%20La%20Vera%20-Caceres.pdf).
- MAPA 1993 trial: best results with selected "Agridulce de La Vera" lines grown by transplanting on ridges (caballones) 75 cm apart — [MAPA Agricultura 1993](https://www.mapa.gob.es/ministerio/pags/Biblioteca/Revistas/pdf_Agri/Agri_1993_733_688_689.pdf).
- MAPA open-air guide: 0.75 m between lines and 20-30 cm between plants depending on variety — [MAPA, cultivo de pimiento para pimentón](https://www.mapa.gob.es/app/MaterialVegetal/docs/cultivo%20de%20pimiento%20para%20piment%C3%B3n.pdf).
- Direct-seeded paprika ('Agridulce SIA') on raised beds in double rows, thinned to 13,333 to >500,000 plants/ha; yield rose with density but only slightly above 200,000 plants/ha (the abstract's "0.35 cm" between double rows is evidently a typo, probably 0.35 m) — [AGRIS: plant density in direct-seeded paprika](https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/65de97df4c5aef494fdc8adf).

**Hungary / Central Europe**
- Hungarian teaching material (context: forcing/soilless and protected pepper, not explicitly field spice paprika): twin-row layout with 40-60 cm between the rows of a pair and 60-90 cm between pairs; plant distance 20-30 cm for single-stem and 28-40 cm for two-stem training — [Szeged EFOP reading, Talaj nélküli termesztés 07](https://eta.bibl.u-szeged.hu/5036/18/EFOP_343_AP6_Talaj%20n%C3%A9lk%C3%BCli%20termeszt%C3%A9s_07_Olvas%C3%B3lecke_Viktor_jav.pdf).
- Another Szeged teaching text gives layouts 70 x 35 cm (4 plants/m²), 80 x 25 cm (5 plants/m²) and twin rows 70 + 50 cm x 42 cm (4 plants/m²) — [Szeged EFOP reading, Ledóné 3](https://eta.bibl.u-szeged.hu/5357/2/EFOP343_AP6MGK_olvas%C3%B3lecke_Led%C3%B3n%C3%A9_3_2021_03_15.pdf).
- Romanian solarium (tunnel) trial: increasing in-row distance from 20 to 30, 40, 50 cm progressively decreased yield/m² in all five paprika genotypes — [Acta, Univ. Szeged repository](https://contentas.ek.szte.hu/Record/acta55447/Details).

**India (chilli)**
- No source with the commonly quoted Indian spacing was retrieved (see Gaps).

### Inferences
- Free strip width between canopy edges (author's arithmetic): row width minus canopy width. With 75 cm rows (Spain, Hungary-type 70-80 cm layouts) and a mature canopy 40-60 cm wide (canopy width not sourced, see Section 2 gaps), a 15-35 cm strip remains at mid-season; with 76-102 cm NM rows, ~16-62 cm. A 15 cm rover with ±3-5 cm steering error needs roughly 20-25 cm, so it fits through mid-season in 90-100 cm rows and is marginal in 70-76 cm rows once plants are full size.
- US plasticulture (author's arithmetic from the guides above): with 152 cm (5 ft) plastic over a twin-row bed (bed top ~91-102 cm if 51-61 cm of plastic goes to shoulders and tuck), the bare row middle is roughly 50-90 cm on 152-198 cm bed centres. A rover fits there all season; inside the bed, between twin rows 30-46 cm apart, the ground is plastic, so there is nothing to weed except planting holes.
- Wheel tracks: in US plasticulture, tractor wheels run in the row middles (inference from bed-centre equals tractor track design); in NM furrow systems wheels run in the furrows. Wheel tracks compact soil and are the most trafficable lanes for a rover.

### Gaps
- No Hungarian field spice paprika row/twin-row spacing for direct-seeded (helyrevetés) vs transplanted crops was retrieved. Hungarian searches returned only forcing/teaching material; the MATE spice paprika document and the Somogyi (2010) dissertation were unreachable (host blocked).
- No Serbian/Macedonian (e.g., Kurtovska kapija, Ajvarka), Turkish (e.g., kapya, Maraş biber) or Chinese spacing data were retrieved.
- No source for Indian chilli spacing (60 x 45 cm is the value usually quoted; unverified here).
- Bed height for NM chile beds and furrow width not retrieved.

## 2. Plant architecture over the season: height, first fork, canopy width, closure, lodging, staking

### Takeaway
Pepper is a short (typically 0.6-1.2 m), bushy, brittle crop whose lowest fruit and branches sit only 10-25 cm above the soil in machine-harvest chile types; bell types have wide fork angles and low, heavy fruit. A 10 cm tall rover can pass under the outer canopy most of the season; a 25 cm tall rover cannot get under the lower branches near the stem. Many fresh peppers are staked and string-tied (Florida weave), which adds stakes and strings along the row.

### Cited Findings
- C. annuum plants are usually 2-4 ft (61-122 cm) tall, most commonly ~3 ft (91 cm) (grower website; home-garden context, lower-quality source) — [PepperGeek](https://peppergeek.com/how-tall-do-pepper-plants-grow/).
- For machine-harvested NM chile, the first fruit set should be roughly 4-10 in (10-25 cm) above ground; a wide fork angle (typical of cherry and bell peppers) can trap and crush fruit; breeders target fewer basal branches near the soil surface to reduce branch breakage — [NMSU CTF13 / NMSU chile mechanisation material](https://pubs.nmsu.edu/research/horticulture/CTF13/index.html); [Walker, IPC 2018](https://conference.ifas.ufl.edu/PEPPER2018/presentations/Open_Session1/0955_Walker%20IPC%202018.pdf); [HortTechnology 2018, mechanical harvest of six NM cultivars](https://journals.ashs.org/view/journals/horttech/28/3/article-p310.xml).
- Increasing density (closer in-row spacing) reduced stem thickness and main fork angle and increased fruit and plant height in chile — [NMSU CTF13](https://pubs.nmsu.edu/research/horticulture/CTF13/index.html); height to first primary branch is a measured breeding trait (from the base of the plant to the first bifurcation, in cm) — [Frontiers in Horticulture 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fhort.2024.1448159/full).
- Plant height significantly affects mechanical harvest; shorter plants yield less marketable fruit by machine — [HortTechnology 2018](https://journals.ashs.org/view/journals/horttech/28/3/article-p310.xml).
- Hungarian spice paprika cultivars are indeterminate (e.g., Borbási), semi-determinate (Kalocsai merevszárú 622, "stiff-stem") or determinate (Kalocsai determinált 601); determinate types can set several flowers per node — [MATE thesis](https://stud.mater.uni-mate.hu/10714/1/823748921.pdf); Szegedi 20 described as medium height with dense foliage — [MAPA Spanish horticulture journal 2003](https://mapa.gob.es/ministerio/pags/Biblioteca/Revistas/pdf%5FSH%2FSH%5F2003%5F11%5F297%5F302%2Epdf).
- Staking: in double rows, one row of stakes per pepper row, tied separately or tied around the outside to box the plants — [Growing Magazine](https://www.growingmagazine.com/?p=2073); Florida-weave stakes are driven 12 in (30 cm) into the ground with one plant between stakes, or 18 in (46 cm) with two plants — [Modern Farmer](https://modernfarmer.com/2016/03/florida-weave-tomato-trellis/).
- Greenhouse/tunnel peppers are pruned to 1-3 stems ("V" system: two stems formed by removing one of the two shoots at each node), restricting branch number — [Queensland HortTechnology paper](https://era.dpi.qld.gov.au/id/eprint/14892/1/horttech-article-p507.pdf).

### Inferences
- Transplant height (~15-20 cm, typical for 6-8 week transplants) and canopy width at maturity (~40-60 cm for bell and field paprika) are general agronomic knowledge, not sourced here.
- Under-canopy clearance: if the first fork/fruit set is 10-25 cm above the soil, the 10 cm rover configuration can drive under the drooping outer canopy and reach near the stem; a 25 cm rover will push through or deflect branches, which risks breakage of brittle pepper branches and fruit drop. On raised beds the furrow floor sits below the bed top, which adds clearance in the furrow (bed height not sourced).
- Fruit-laden branches of bell and paprika types droop and lodge into the alley in the second half of the season (common field observation, not sourced here); stakes and strings in staked systems give a fixed, regular row edge, which is easier to follow.
- Canopy closure: with 70-76 cm rows, canopies likely meet by mid-season (roughly 8-12 weeks after transplanting); with 90-100 cm rows or plasticulture row middles a gap usually remains. This is inference; no sourced closure date was found.

### Gaps
- No measured first-fork height, canopy width or canopy closure date for Hungarian spice paprika or bell pepper was retrieved; the Florida State Horticultural Society architecture paper measured stem height to the first main branch node, but its numbers were not in the extract — [FSHS](https://journals.flvc.org/fshs/article/download/114733/110060/160921).
- No data on fallen fruit/leaf litter amounts.

## 3. Ground conditions: flat vs ridge vs raised bed, mulch, drip tape, furrows, soils, debris

### Takeaway
The rover would meet three different ground surfaces: (1) black plastic mulch over a raised bed with drip tape (nothing to weed except planting holes; risk of tearing plastic), (2) bare soil row middles between mulched beds (the real weeding zone in plasticulture), and (3) bare furrow-irrigated ridges/beds (NM chile, La Vera ridges 75 cm), which are wet and muddy after irrigation and crust when dry. Raised dome beds of at least 23 cm are recommended for Phytophthora control.

### Cited Findings
- Black polyethylene mulch is the most used option in Spanish pepper because it prevents weeds in the crop line and cuts water use; clear plastic does not prevent weeds — [MAPA pimentón guide](https://www.mapa.gob.es/app/MaterialVegetal/docs/cultivo%20de%20pimiento%20para%20piment%C3%B3n.pdf); MAPA also recommends either mulch or a shallow cultivation, which also breaks the soil crust — [MAPA Vida Rural 2001](https://mapa.gob.es/ministerio/pags/Biblioteca/Revistas/pdf%5Fvrural%2FVrural%5F2001%5F124%5F46%5F49%2Epdf).
- Pimentón in La Vera grown transplanted on ridges (caballones) 75 cm apart — [MAPA 1993](https://www.mapa.gob.es/ministerio/pags/Biblioteca/Revistas/pdf_Agri/Agri_1993_733_688_689.pdf).
- NM chile is furrow- or drip-irrigated on raised beds; flumioxazin applied to furrows did not reduce yield on coarse soil, but on raised beds it reduced yield of some cultivars in 2015 and 2017 (shows the bed vs furrow distinction in NM) — [Weed Technology, flumioxazin in NM chile](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/97888CD8AE9DF9B30CA30CCDD7D7F339).
- Phytophthora management: dome-shaped raised beds at least 9 in (23 cm) high; transplanter should not leave a depression around the plant base — [UMass Extension](https://www.umass.edu/agriculture-food-environment/vegetable/fact-sheets/phytophthora-blight); use a bed shaper to build stable beds, "do not build a loose ridge"; create breaks in beds so water can leave the field; avoid over-irrigation; do not enter fields with wet foliage — [Delaware Weekly Crop Update checklist](https://sites.udel.edu/weeklycropupdate/?p=5721); grade fields to prevent standing water — [Kentucky PPFS-VG-04](https://publications.ca.uky.edu/sites/publications.ca.uky.edu/files/PPFS-VG-04.pdf).
- Row middles between plastic-mulched beds are bare soil managed with herbicides (pre and post), cultivation, mowing, rye dead mulch or living mulches; organic bell pepper trial: total yield fell ~30% in all between-row (row-middle) treatments vs weed-free cultivation — [Specialty Crop Grower, row-middle weed control](https://specialtycropgrower.com/specialty-crop-grower-magazine-weeds-plasticulture-row-middles).
- Deep cultivation (9 cm) reduced bell pepper productivity vs shallow (3 cm), because it prunes roots and dries the soil — [HortScience article](https://journals.ashs.org/downloadpdf/view/journals/hortsci/36/1/article-p66.pdf) (as summarised in search result; exact paper attribution between the two HortScience PDFs returned is uncertain, see also [HortScience 22(3):418](https://journals.ashs.org/downloadpdf/view/journals/hortsci/22/3/article-p418.pdf)).

### Inferences
- In plasticulture, drip tape is under the plastic on the bed (standard practice), so a rover in the row middle does not cross it; in bare-soil drip systems (e.g., Hungarian/Spanish field drip) tape lies on or just under the surface beside the row, where a rover wheel or a hoe blade can cut or snag it.
- Furrow-irrigated fields (NM, parts of Spain/Turkey/Balkans) are untrafficable for a 15 cm wheeled robot for ~1-3 days after each irrigation and have 10-25 cm height steps between furrow and bed (bed height not sourced; inference).
- Soil types: clay-loam and silt soils of the Hungarian Great Plain (Szeged/Kalocsa) crust and crack after irrigation; not sourced here.

### Gaps
- No sourced data on Hungarian spice paprika soil types, crusting or ground roughness, nor on mulch adoption share in Hungarian field pepper.

## 4. Weed flora and emergence

### Takeaway
Pepper fields are dominated by summer annuals (Amaranthus, Chenopodium, Echinochloa, Setaria, Portulaca, Datura, Ambrosia in Hungary) plus perennials (nutsedges Cyperus spp., field bindweed Convolvulus arvensis) that a small mechanical robot cannot kill by cutting. Late-emerging weeds after crop thinning (e.g., spurred anoda in NM) still cause large yield losses, so weeding is needed through mid-season.

### Cited Findings
- Hungarian row crops share a common dominant flora: Echinochloa crus-galli, Ambrosia artemisiifolia and Chenopodium album are the three most dominant weeds in Hungarian maize; Echinochloa, Chenopodium album, Portulaca oleracea and Hibiscus trionum were the most prevalent taxa in eastern Hungarian maize 2018-2021 — [Agronomy 2025, 15(8):1814](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4395/15/8/1814/htm); Chenopodium album, Amaranthus retroflexus, Ambrosia artemisiifolia and annual grasses (Echinochloa, Setaria) are common in 31 SE-Hungarian pea fields — [Agronomy 2023, 13(7):1864](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4395/13/7/1864/html).
- National survey of 180 Hungarian oil-pumpkin fields: high seeding rate and crop cover suppressed Amaranthus retroflexus; cultivating tillage reduced Ambrosia artemisiifolia and Setaria pumila — [AGRIS: oil pumpkin weed survey](https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/65dfd4f17c7033e84bef2a47).
- NM chile: field bindweed and nutsedge are managed largely through rotation with cotton and field corn, which have better herbicide options; hand hoeing is required to remove weed species not managed by other means — [NMSU CR548](https://pubs.nmsu.edu/_circulars/CR548/index.html) (search extract).
- Broadleaf weeds are a primary challenge in NM chile, partly because few broadleaf herbicides are available — [Weed Science, NM chile](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/5D10A731699A1159652287ED784797D0/core-reader).
- Spurred anoda (Anoda cristata) emerging after thinning reduced chile yield 31-49% (plants thinned to 10 cm) and 12-27% (thinned to 20 cm) at the highest anoda density — [Weed Technology 2023](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/976115DABDDFAC0957092D6C02380C2F/core-reader).
- Purple nutsedge in Florida bell pepper: season-long interference at 20-80 plants/m² caused ~30-70% marketable yield loss — [Proc. Fla. State Hort. Soc. 116 (2003)](https://journals.flvc.org/fshs/article/view/86512).
- Yellow nutsedge-free period needed: 3-5 weeks in spring-grown and 1-7 weeks in fall-grown peppers — [Weed Technology, imazosulfuron in bell pepper](https://www-cambridge-org.ezproxyberklee.flo.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/789409DAACE41B1F118E1ECE3CA1795D/S0890037X00013622a.pdf/bell-pepper-capsicum-annuum-tolerance-to-imazosulfuron-and-thifensulfuron-methyl.pdf).
- Nutsedges penetrate plastic mulch (implied by Florida nutsedge-under-mulch research above and by row-middle guidance) — [Proc. Fla. State Hort. Soc. 116](https://journals.flvc.org/fshs/article/view/86512).

### Inferences
- Hungarian pepper flora is very probably the same as these row crops (Echinochloa, Chenopodium, Amaranthus, Ambrosia, Setaria, Portulaca, Hibiscus trionum, Datura), since pepper is grown in the same regions and rotations; no pepper-specific Hungarian survey was found.
- Perennials (Convolvulus arvensis, Cirsium arvense, Cyperus rotundus/esculentus) regrow from rhizomes/tubers after shallow cutting; a small robot would only suppress them by repeated cutting, not kill them.
- Emergence: summer annuals emerge in flushes after each irrigation or rain from May to July in Central Europe; repeated passes every 7-14 days would be needed (inference from CPWC durations and irrigation cycles).

### Gaps
- No Hungarian or Balkan pepper-specific weed survey; no emergence-timing curves for pepper fields; parasitic weeds (Orobanche/Phelipanche, Cuscuta) in pepper not searched.

## 5. Critical period for weed control (CPWC) and yield loss

### Takeaway
Transplanted pepper needs to be kept weed-free from about 1-2 weeks to 5-9 weeks after transplanting (up to ~13 weeks with strict 5% loss thresholds); direct-seeded chile and paprika need a longer window, starting at emergence, because the crop establishes slowly. Season-long weed competition costs 30-85% of yield.

### Cited Findings
- Brazil (sweet pepper): with a 5% loss tolerance, control needed from 11 to 89 days after transplanting; season-long weeds caused up to ~85% loss — [Planta Daninha 2018 (DOAJ)](https://doaj.org/article/2679da1463d34004a90557382385cc5e).
- Egypt: weed management recommended for ~8 weeks after transplanting (quadratic models gave 8.3 and 7.0 weeks); the paper cites a broader literature range of 6.7-15.3 weeks for a 5% loss threshold — [Egyptian journal article 79106](https://journals.ekb.eg/article_79106.html).
- Puerto Rico: critical competition period from transplanting to about the first 5 weeks — [J. Agric. Univ. Puerto Rico](https://revistas.upr.edu/index.php/jaupr/article/download/3625/3121/3733).
- Florida purple nutsedge: suppressing nutsedge during the first 6 weeks after transplanting kept yield loss near 5% — [Proc. Fla. State Hort. Soc. 116](https://journals.flvc.org/fshs/article/view/86512).
- Transplanted chile pepper CPWC up to 126 days (Amador-Ramirez 2002), and likely longer in direct-seeded chile; chile is slow to establish and susceptible to early-season competition — [Weed Technology 2023](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/976115DABDDFAC0957092D6C02380C2F/core-reader).
- India (chilli): critical period of crop-weed competition ~30-45 days after planting; 60-70% yield loss if weeds not controlled — [ICAR Indian Farming](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IndFarm/article/download/85365/35045/218230); Gujarat: weed losses 60-70%, unweeded check up to 75.2% yield reduction in kharif — [Indian J. Weed Science 2015](https://isws.org.in/IJWSn/File/2015_47_Issue-4_390-392.pdf); Rajasthan: 60-80% loss — [Indian J. Weed Science 2004](https://isws.org.in/IJWSn/File/2004_36_Issue-3&4_300-301.pdf).

### Inferences
- Weeding frequency: to hold a crop weed-free for 5-9 weeks with flushes after each irrigation, an autonomous weeder would need to pass every 7-10 days (6-10 passes per season), and late flushes (post-thinning anoda, nutsedge) argue for continuing to ~12 weeks. This is inference.
- The CPWC falls mostly before canopy closure, when the rover is not needed "under" the canopy; the under-canopy niche is the late part of the CPWC and after-CPWC weed seed-set prevention.

### Gaps
- Tursun et al. (2012, direct-seeded vs transplanted pepper CPWC, Turkey) was named in search results but not retrieved; no Hungarian CPWC for spice paprika found.

## 6. Current weed management, costs, and the inter-row vs intra-row split

### Takeaway
Weed control in pepper is a combination of pre-emergence herbicides (few registered, especially in the EU), mechanical inter-row cultivation by tractor until lay-by, plastic mulch on the bed, and expensive hand hoeing for the in-row/near-stem zone. Hand labour for the intra-row zone is the dominant weeding cost (US NM: savings of $262-440/acre from reducing hoeing alone). An inter-row-only robot addresses the cheaper part of the problem unless it can work close to stems or replaces row-middle herbicides in plasticulture/organic systems.

### Cited Findings
**Hand weeding and cost**
- NM chile: managing weeds in the chile row requires hand hoeing for species not managed otherwise; close cultivation before thinning lets hoe crews move faster; cultivation that moves soil around the plant base when chile is ~5 in (13 cm) tall gives some in-row control — [NMSU CR548](https://pubs.nmsu.edu/_circulars/CR548/index.html) (search extract).
- NM: hand-hoeing labour costs are "often prohibitively expensive" for southern NM farms; hand weeding significantly reduces chile profitability — [Weed Science, NM chile](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/5D10A731699A1159652287ED784797D0); barley cover crops reduced hoeing time in 3 of 4 site-years without yield loss; brown mustard did not reduce hoeing time — [Weed Science 2024, barley vs mustard](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/973B6BD414D2CC182ABF30E5BD72FF43/core-reader).
- False seedbeds reduced hand-hoeing costs by $262-440/acre (~$650-1,090/ha) net of implementation costs and improved profitability — [HortTechnology 2021, false seedbeds in chile](https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/65df6bf27c7033e84beda4ac).
- UK (Wales EIP robotic weeder report, field vegetables, not pepper): hand weeding costs up to £1,000-2,000/ha and may be needed twice or more per year — [EIP Wales robotic weeder report](https://businesswales.gov.wales/farmingconnect/sites/farmingconnect/files/documents/EIP%20Wales_Robotic%20weeder%20final%20report.pdf).
- Puerto Rico (1988): total labour for drip-irrigated, plastic-mulched pepper ~1,964 man-hours/ha (all operations, not weeding alone) — [J. Agric. Univ. Puerto Rico](https://revistas.upr.edu/index.php/jaupr/article/download/6832/5483/6397).
- India: three hand weedings plus hoeing gave the highest net return in Gujarat chilli; pre-emergence pendimethalin plus one hand weeding at 45 DAT matched two hand weedings in Maharashtra; labour shortages for hand weeding are worsened by rural employment-guarantee wages — [Indian J. Weed Science 2015](https://isws.org.in/IJWSn/File/2015_47_Issue-4_390-392.pdf); [ICAR Indian Farming](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IndFarm/article/download/85367/35046/218234).

**Herbicides**
- US NM chile: pendimethalin conventionally applied after thinning (9-10 weeks after seeding); post-directed pendimethalin at 5 weeks after seeding caused no injury or yield loss — [Weed Technology 2023](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/976115DABDDFAC0957092D6C02380C2F/core-reader).
- US row middles in plasticulture: glufosinate supplemental label allows max 2 row-middle applications per season, max 62 fl oz/acre (~4.5 L/ha) per season, 30 days suggested interval for pepper; Florida tank mixes: control rose from 42-73% (S-metolachlor or flumioxazin alone) to 91% (both); consistent mixes are paraquat + flumioxazin, S-metolachlor + flumioxazin, S-metolachlor + oxyfluorfen; pepper-specific: clethodim for grasses, imazosulfuron directed at the base for broadleaves/nutsedge; burn-down with carfentrazone, paraquat, diquat; smart (detect-and-spray) row-middle sprayers have been studied — [Specialty Crop Grower](https://specialtycropgrower.com/specialty-crop-grower-magazine-weeds-plasticulture-row-middles); [Weed Technology, row-middle herbicides](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/weed-technology/article/pre-and-postemergence-herbicides-for-row-middle-weed-control-in-vegetable-plasticulture-production-systems/FE2242EBA6D42E6BC28B7382A775BD20).
- Bell pepper tolerance to PRE herbicides applied post-transplant in plasticulture has been studied (Weed Technology 2022) — [Weed Technology 2022](https://cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/5CB32A42554F78C346E1C9FC9E5F289E/S0890037X22000987a.pdf/tomato-and-bell-pepper-tolerance-to-preemergence-herbicides-applied-posttransplant-in-plasticulture-production.pdf); napropamide + hand weeding economics in bell pepper — [Weed Technology](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/weed-technology/article/weed-control-economics-in-bell-pepper-capsicum-annuum-with-napropamide-and-hand-weeding/181F4D4BB2B6EBF7B402243166F69781).
- Hungary: Nébih decision (Aug 2025) authorises FMC's Reactor Micro in spice paprika after sowing, before emergence (BBCH 00-05) against annual monocot and dicot weeds from seed — [Nébih/FMC Reactor Micro decision 2025](https://ag.fmc.com/hu/sites/default/files/2025-08/Reactor%20Micro%20hivatalbol%20mod%202025.08.12_alairt.pdf); a 2023 Szeged thesis studies chemical weed control in irrigated spice paprika — [Nánai 2023](https://diploma.bibl.u-szeged.hu/id/eprint/142656/).
- EU: clomazone approval period extended to 31 Oct 2027 — [COLEAD EU approval changes 2025](https://resources.colead.link/sites/default/files/file_fields/2026/R&IB/Approvals/EN%20_Table%201.%20EU%20all%20Approval%20changes%202025.pdf); a clomazone + pendimethalin product received a French extension of use (southern zone) — [ANSES](https://www.anses.fr/system/files/phyto/evaluations/STALLIONSY_PMAJ_2013-0289_PARTA.pdf).

### Inferences
- Reactor is FMC's clomazone brand, so the Hungarian spice paprika pre-emergence product is probably clomazone-based (inference; active ingredient not shown in the extract).
- Inter-row vs intra-row split: in both NM chile and EU field pepper, tractor cultivation already handles the inter-row cheaply until lay-by; the residual, expensive job is in-row/near-stem hand hoeing. No source quantifies the split; author's estimate is that the intra-row zone accounts for most (perhaps 60-90%) of hand-weeding hours, since inter-row hoeing by tractor costs only a few tens of EUR/ha per pass. This makes a strictly inter-row robot low-value in conventional open field, and more valuable where (a) organic growers cannot use herbicides in row middles, (b) canopy overhang blocks tractors late in the season, or (c) the robot can weed within ~5 cm of stems.
- In plasticulture, the robot's job is the bare row middle (where glufosinate is capped at 2 applications/season) and planting-hole weeds; it competes with cheap directed sprays and mowing.

### Gaps
- No European (Hungarian, Spanish) figure for hand-weeding hours/ha in pepper; no Indian man-days/ha; no current NMSU enterprise budget with hoeing $/acre; no EU-wide list of herbicides registered in pepper (EU Pesticides Database not reached); S-metolachlor EU non-renewal status not verified in this session.

## 7. Crop sensitivity: roots, stem brittleness, disease spread by tools/wheels, drip tape

### Takeaway
Pepper roots are concentrated in the top ~20 cm with lateral spread up to 75 cm, and deep cultivation (9 cm) cuts yield vs shallow (3 cm), so any tool should work at ≤3 cm depth. The bigger risk is disease: tobamoviruses (ToBRFV and, by family, PMMoV/TMV) spread on hands, tools, equipment and trellis ropes and persist for months to years; Phytophthora capsici moves with soil and water and survives >10 years. A robot that touches foliage or carries soil between fields is a potential vector.

### Cited Findings
- Drip-irrigated pepper: root length density fell with depth, rapidly below 20 cm, with little horizontal variation — [Agronomy 15:02822](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/agronomy/agronomy-15-02822/article_deploy/agronomy-15-02822.pdf) or [Frontiers 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/plant-science/articles/10.3389/fpls.2025.1589560/pdf) (search extract did not specify which of the two).
- Older review: pepper roots spread horizontally to 75 cm from the crown, some reach 90-120 cm depth; 80% of the active root system in the upper 75 cm (Hammes & Bartz 1963) — [HortScience 36(1):66 or 22(3):418](https://journals.ashs.org/downloadpdf/view/journals/hortsci/36/1/article-p66.pdf); highest percentage of roots in the top 600 mm — [Starke Ayres pepper guideline 2019](https://www.starkeayres.com/uploads/files/Sweet-Hot-Pepper-Production-Guideline-2019.pdf).
- Deep (9 cm) vs shallow (3 cm) cultivation: deep cultivation decreased bell pepper productivity, pruning roots and drying soil — [HortScience](https://journals.ashs.org/downloadpdf/view/journals/hortsci/36/1/article-p66.pdf).
- Tobamoviruses (ToBRFV) move between plants on workers' hands, clothes, tools including knives, equipment including trellising ropes — [EPPO Global Database ToBRFV](https://gd.eppo.int/taxon/TOBRFV/datasheet); routes include contaminated tools, equipment, clothing, hands, crop debris in soil, irrigation water and plant contact — [Purdue CAPS ToBRFV datasheet 2024](https://caps.ceris.purdue.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Tomato-brown-rugose-fruit-virus-datasheet-2024.pdf); brushing plants can move ToBRFV; tobamoviruses may remain infectious in debris, soil and surfaces for >20 years (extrapolated from the genus) — [Cornell factsheet](https://www.vegetables.cornell.edu/pest-management/disease-factsheets/tomato-brown-rugose-fruit-virus); survives on hands/gloves ≥2 h and greenhouse surfaces up to 6 months; recommendation to disinfect hands and equipment between plants — [Biobest ToBRFV update](https://academy.biobestgroup.com/blogs/news/tobrfv-update); ToBRFV is a concern for pepper as well as tomato — [Purdue Vegetable Crops Hotline](https://vegcropshotline.org/?p=8499).
- Phytophthora capsici: spread by moving contaminated soil and surface irrigation water; survives in soil >10 years; plastic mulch alone does not reduce blight; do not enter fields with wet foliage — [UVM Extension](https://www.uvm.edu/vtvegandberry/factsheets/phytophthora.html); [Kentucky PPFS-VG-04](https://publications.ca.uky.edu/sites/publications.ca.uky.edu/files/PPFS-VG-04.pdf); [Delaware checklist](https://sites.udel.edu/weeklycropupdate/?p=5721).
- Row-middle weeds host insects, nematodes and pathogens — [Specialty Crop Grower](https://specialtycropgrower.com/specialty-crop-grower-magazine-weeds-plasticulture-row-middles).

### Inferences
- PMMoV and TMV are tobamoviruses with the same mechanical transmission biology as ToBRFV (general plant-pathology knowledge; no PMMoV-specific source retrieved). A rover brushing pepper leaves under the canopy, especially a 25 cm tall body, could act like a worker's hands/clothing; design should avoid foliage contact, and wheels/tools should be cleanable between fields/greenhouses.
- Root safety: tools working within ~3 cm depth and no closer than the stem's lateral root zone near the base are implied; pepper stems and branches are brittle (common knowledge; no sourced breaking force), so collisions with the main stem must be avoided.
- Drip tape in bare-soil systems is a cut risk for blades (inference).

### Gaps
- No quantified stem breaking force or branch brittleness for pepper; no PMMoV-specific transmission data; no bacterial spot (Xanthomonas) spread-by-equipment data retrieved.

## 8. Protected cultivation (polytunnels, unheated greenhouses, soil-grown)

### Takeaway
Hungary's largest forced vegetable is fresh pepper (~1,500 ha), much of it soil-grown in unheated or lightly heated plastic tunnels at 4-8 plants/m² in single or twin rows (e.g., 70 x 35 cm; 70 + 50 cm x 42 cm), often string-trained to 1-3 stems. Paths (50-90 cm) are wide enough for the rover, but beds are commonly mulched, plants are trained vertically, climate is hot and humid, GPS is unreliable under film, and tobamovirus hygiene is critical. Substrate/hydroponic pepper (Almería, NW Europe) has essentially no weeds.

### Cited Findings
- Fresh (étkezési) pepper is Hungary's largest forced vegetable at nearly 1,500 ha — Hungarian university teaching material returned in the salvaged search (exact document among [Szeged EFOP reading Ledóné 2](https://eta.bibl.u-szeged.hu/5357/1/EFOP343_AP6MGK_olvas%C3%B3lecke_Led%C3%B3n%C3%A9_2_2021_03_15.pdf) / [Ledóné 4](https://eta.bibl.u-szeged.hu/5357/3/EFOP343_AP6MGK_olvas%C3%B3lecke_Led%C3%B3n%C3%A9_4_2021_03_15.pdf) not identified).
- Densities: traditional cold (unheated) soil forcing 6-8 plants/m²; lightly heated 5-7 plants/m²; two-stem training 3-3.5 plants/m²; three-stem 2-3 plants/m²; layouts 70 x 35 cm (4/m²), 80 x 25 cm (5/m²), twin rows 70 + 50 cm x 42 cm (4/m²); in-row 20-25 cm — [Szeged EFOP reading Ledóné 3](https://eta.bibl.u-szeged.hu/5357/2/EFOP343_AP6MGK_olvas%C3%B3lecke_Led%C3%B3n%C3%A9_3_2021_03_15.pdf).
- Rijk Zwaan 2026 Hungarian pepper catalogue: two-stem 3.0-3.5 plants/m², one-stem 6.0-7.0 plants/m², unheated 7.5-8.0 stems/m²; do not exceed 3.5 plants/m² in cordon (trellis) growing — [Rijk Zwaan Paprika katalógus 2026](https://visuals.rijkzwaan.com/m/c5c9c97146ea7b33/original/Paprika-katalogus-2026.pdf); 2024 catalogue similar (6.5-7 one-stem, 3.0-3.5 two-stem) — [Rijk Zwaan 2024](https://visuals.rijkzwaan.com/m/642cb92f270731d/original/HU_Paprika_fajtaajanlat_2024_web.pdf).
- Twin-row protected layout: 40-60 cm within the pair, 60-90 cm between pairs; 20-30 cm in-row (single stem), 28-40 cm (two stems) — [Szeged EFOP Talaj nélküli 07](https://eta.bibl.u-szeged.hu/5036/18/EFOP_343_AP6_Talaj%20n%C3%A9lk%C3%BCli%20termeszt%C3%A9s_07_Olvas%C3%B3lecke_Viktor_jav.pdf).
- Research tunnel example: 350 m² unheated plastic tunnel at the Szent István University experimental farm, soil vs soilless pepper comparison — [Csapó-Birkás PhD theses, MATE](https://phd.mater.uni-mate.hu/75/2/csapo-birkas_zita-tezis_DOI.pdf).
- Spanish protected pepper increasingly in substrate — [Cajamar, producción de pimiento en sustrato](https://publicacionescajamar.es/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/produccion-de-pimiento-en-sustrato.pdf).
- US high tunnels: two rows per bed 14 in apart, trellised (Florida weave) — [Richland County SC](https://richlandcountysc.gov/Portals/0/Departments/Conservation/DistrictDocs/High%20Tunnel%202017/Spring%20Warm%20Season%20Crops%20in%20High%20Tunnel.pdf).
- ToBRFV survives on greenhouse surfaces up to 6 months; trellising ropes are a transmission route — [Biobest](https://academy.biobestgroup.com/blogs/news/tobrfv-update); [EPPO](https://gd.eppo.int/taxon/TOBRFV/datasheet).

### Inferences
- Rover fit in tunnels: 60-90 cm paths between twin rows and 40-60 cm within pairs both exceed 15 cm all season; string-trained single-stem plants have bare lower stems once lower leaves and side shoots are removed, which gives clear under-canopy space at 10-25 cm height. Geometry is the most favourable of all pepper systems.
- Usefulness is limited because tunnel beds are usually covered with black PE mulch or ground-cover fabric and paths are often covered or trampled (common practice, not sourced here); weed pressure is then confined to planting holes and path edges. Bare-soil tunnels (low-input family farms) would benefit most.
- GNSS/RTK is degraded under plastic film and metal hoops; navigation would need row-following by vision/LiDAR or wire/marker guidance (inference).
- Tunnel climate: hot (often >35 °C in summer) and humid; electronics cooling and condensation are design constraints (inference).

### Gaps
- No sourced data on path widths in Hungarian commercial tunnels, ground-cover adoption share, or weed pressure in soil-grown tunnel pepper.

## 9. Scale and economics

### Takeaway
Hungarian spice paprika has collapsed from ~9,000 ha (1990) to ~1,000-2,000 ha (2019-2023), with Kalocsa at ~310 ha; Hungarian forced fresh pepper is ~1,500 ha. Worldwide, green chillies and peppers cover ~2.1 Mha (2023). Labour cost and availability of hand weeders are cited as profitability constraints in the US and India. No source gives what growers would pay for robotic weeding; the best proxies are hand-hoeing cost savings ($650-1,090/ha in NM) and UK hand weeding costs (£1,000-2,000/ha).

### Cited Findings
- Hungary spice paprika area (KSH via Telex): 9,000 ha in 1990, 1,910 ha in 2019, 1,062 ha "last year" (i.e., 2023) — [Telex, 2 Dec 2024](https://telex.hu/gazdasag/2024/12/02/egyre-kevesebbet-terem-a-szegedi-fuszerpaprika); a 2023 Hungarian article puts it at 1,800-2,000 ha, divided between the Kalocsa and Szeged districts (the search summary did not identify which result URL; it conflicts with the KSH figure and should be treated as unverified); Kalocsa: 3,000 ha around 2000, now 310 ha — [Világgazdaság, Oct 2025](https://www.vg.hu/vilaggazdasag-magyar-gazdasag/2025/10/kalocsai-fuszerpaprika-ermeles); older press: decline from 10-14 thousand ha to 3-4 thousand ha — [Index](https://index.hu/gazdasag/2019/04/06/magyar_fuszerpaprika_kinai_paprika_olcso); government support of HUF 500 million for paprika growers — [Privátbankár](https://privatbankar.hu/cikkek/agrar/felpaprikazzak-a-termeloket-500-milliot-ad-a-kormany-325076.html).
- Global green chillies and peppers harvested area peaked at ~2.1 Mha in 2023 (third-party summary of FAO data) — [IndexBox](https://www.indexbox.io/blog/chili-and-pepper-world-market-overview-2024-5/); FAOSTAT's green item covers all Capsicum (bell included) and dry chillies are a separate item (code 689) — [DBnomics FAO QCL](https://db.nomics.world/FAO/QCL?offset=20).
- US: false seedbeds cut hoeing costs $262-440/acre (see Section 6); hand hoeing "often prohibitively expensive" in southern NM — [Weed Science](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/5D10A731699A1159652287ED784797D0).
- UK field vegetables: hand weeding £1,000-2,000/ha, possibly twice or more per year — [EIP Wales](https://businesswales.gov.wales/farmingconnect/sites/farmingconnect/files/documents/EIP%20Wales_Robotic%20weeder%20final%20report.pdf).
- India: labour shortage for hand weeding linked to rural employment-guarantee wages — [ICAR Indian Farming](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IndFarm/article/download/85367/35046/218234).

### Inferences
- Hungarian spice paprika is a small, shrinking, fragmented niche (~1,000-2,000 ha), so a paprika-only robot has a tiny addressable market in Hungary; EU pepper (Spain, Italy, Romania, Hungary, Netherlands greenhouse) and global chilli (~2 Mha green plus dry) are larger, but dominant producers (China, India, Mexico, Turkey) have low labour costs or plasticulture systems.
- Willingness to pay (inference): a robot that removes most in-row hand hoeing could be worth up to the hoeing cost avoided (~EUR 500-2,000/ha/season in EU/US); an inter-row-only robot is worth much less (comparable to tractor cultivation or 1-2 herbicide passes, i.e., tens to low hundreds of EUR/ha).

### Gaps
- No FAOSTAT country figures (China, India, Turkey, Mexico, Spain, Hungary) for 2023/2024 were retrieved; no Eurostat EU pepper area; no value-per-hectare or gross margin for Hungarian paprika or EU pepper; no farm-size distribution.

## 10. Other row crops: which suit a 15 cm wide under-canopy rover

### Takeaway
Crops with wide rows (≥70 cm), tall canopies and lower stems free of foliage (maize, sunflower, wide-row soybean, vineyards, staked/trellised vegetables) offer a persistent under-canopy corridor; crops with rosettes or sprawling foliage at ground level (sugar beet, brassicas, lettuce, potato after closure, carrot, strawberries) close the inter-row and leave only a pre-closure window. Pepper sits in the middle: wide enough rows, but low branches and fruit.

### Cited Findings
- Sugar beet inter-row hoes are designed for 45-50 cm spacing — [Bednar](https://www.bednar.com/en/blog/which-weeder-to-choose-for-your-farm/); [Farmers Weekly, camera-guided sugar beet hoes](https://www.fwi.co.uk/machinery/root-crop-equipment/buyers-guide-camera-guided-sugar-beet-hoes).

## 11. Synthesis: when and where a 15 cm wide, 20 cm long, 10-25 cm tall rover physically fits and is useful in pepper

### Takeaway
Physically, the rover fits between rows in every pepper system examined, because row/alley widths (50-100 cm) are several times its 15 cm width; the binding constraints are low branches/fruit at 10-25 cm (forcing the 10-12 cm low-profile configuration late in the season), muddy furrows after irrigation, plastic mulch edges, and disease hygiene. Usefulness is highest in bare-soil, direct-seeded or transplanted open-field paprika/chile during weeks 2-12 after planting, and in organic plasticulture row middles; it is lowest in mulched tunnels and hydroponics.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
Per-system verdict (author's synthesis):
- **Hungarian / Central European open-field spice paprika and fresh pepper (bare soil, 70-80 cm rows or twin rows, drip or sprinkler; direct-seeded or transplanted):** Fits from emergence/transplanting until canopy edges meet (inferred ~8-12 weeks); after that only the 10-12 cm configuration can pass under drooping branches, and only in rows ≥75 cm. Useful during the whole CPWC; most valuable if it can weed within ~5 cm of stems, since tractor hoes already cover the inter-row. Watch for drip tape on the surface and crusted/cracked clay soils.
- **Spanish pimentón (La Vera ridges 75 cm, Ebro 73-76 cm lines):** As above; ridge slopes and furrow irrigation make the furrow floor the travel lane; untrafficable for a few days after irrigation.
- **Balkan (Serbia, North Macedonia, Bulgaria) and Turkish field pepper:** Geometry not retrieved; likely similar 70-80 cm rows with furrow or drip irrigation (inference only).
- **US plasticulture bell pepper (twin rows on mulched beds):** Fits all season in 50-90 cm bare row middles; inside the bed there is no bare soil to weed. Useful as a non-chemical row-middle weeder (organic, or to replace the 2-application glufosinate cap and drift-prone paraquat), and potentially for planting-hole weeds if it can work right at the stem. Must not tear plastic edges.
- **New Mexico chile (direct-seeded, 76-102 cm beds, furrow irrigation, thinning at 9-10 weeks):** Fits in furrows and on bed shoulders all season; the expensive job is in-row hand hoeing around thinned plants ($650-1,090/ha savings were possible with false seedbeds), so value depends on intra-row capability. Furrows are wet after irrigation; flumioxazin/pendimethalin directed sprays are the competitor.
- **India/China chilli (smallholder, hand-weeded, 30-45 DAP critical period):** Geometry probably fits (~60 cm rows, unverified), but very low labour cost and small plots make an autonomous robot uneconomic except where labour is scarce (inference).
- **Polytunnels / unheated greenhouses, soil-grown (Hungary ~1,500 ha forced pepper):** Fits all season (paths 60-90 cm, bare lower stems on string-trained plants); low weed pressure when beds are mulched; no GNSS; very strict tobamovirus hygiene needed. Moderate usefulness on bare-soil family-farm tunnels.
- **Hydroponic / substrate pepper:** No weeds; not a market.

### Gaps
- Canopy closure timing and measured under-canopy clearance through the season for field paprika remain the key unmeasured parameters; a field survey (photos with a height scale at 2-week intervals) would close this gap.
